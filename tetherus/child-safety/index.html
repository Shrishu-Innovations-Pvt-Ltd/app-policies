Child Safety Standards

Tether Us · Shrishu Innovations Private Limited · Effective date: 5 October 2026 · Last updated: 5 October 2026

Our position in one line. Tether Us has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE). It is prohibited on the app and on any part of our service. We remove it, close the accounts involved, and report it to the authorities.

1. Scope

These standards apply to Tether Us, a private app for two connected partners that offers chat, voice messages, shared drawings, audio and video calls, and optional location sharing. They cover all content and behaviour in the app: text messages, voice messages, drawings, shared locations, call content, profile details and any other material a user sends or shares.

2. What is prohibited

The following is strictly prohibited on Tether Us:

  • Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) of any kind, including images, video, audio, drawings, text or any other content that depicts, describes or promotes the sexual abuse or exploitation of a child.
  • The sexualisation of minors, including sexual content or conversation involving a person under 18.
  • Grooming, which means befriending or building a relationship with a child in order to facilitate sexual contact, either online or offline, or the exchange of sexual imagery.
  • Sextortion, meaning threatening or coercing a person, including a child, using sexual content or the threat of sharing it.
  • Trafficking of children, and any attempt to arrange or advertise the abuse or exploitation of a child.
  • Sharing, requesting, linking to or offering to trade any of the above, by any means in the app.

3. Who may use Tether Us

Tether Us is for adults aged 18 and over. Minors may not create an account or use the app. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 18, we will close it and delete the associated data, except where the law requires us to preserve information.

4. How the app is designed

  • Tether Us has no public profiles, no public feed, no search for other users and no open messaging with strangers.
  • Accounts connect one-to-one, using a 6-digit pairing code (which expires after ten minutes) or a registered email address.
  • Content is shared only between the two connected partners.

Because conversations are private, we do not routinely scan or monitor them. We act on reports we receive, notices from Google Play, and valid requests from law enforcement.

5. How to report a concern

Anyone can report a child safety concern, whether or not they use the app. Please email shrishuinnovations@gmail.com with the subject line "Tether Us child safety report". Include as much as you can:

  • what you saw or experienced, and when;
  • the name or email address of the account involved, if you know it;
  • screenshots, if you can safely provide them. Do not forward or share any material that depicts the abuse of a child. Describe it instead, and we will tell you how to proceed.

Reports can be made confidentially. We do not tell the reported person who made a report unless the law requires it.

6. What we do when we receive a report

  1. We acknowledge the report within 72 hours.
  2. We review it promptly and, where there is actual knowledge or a credible indication of CSAE, we act immediately.
  3. We remove the content and restrict, suspend or permanently close the account or accounts involved.
  4. We preserve the relevant information as the law requires, and we do not share it except with the authorities.
  5. We report confirmed child sexual abuse material and related offences to the relevant authorities, as described in Section 7.
  6. We cooperate with law enforcement and with Google Play on valid requests.

We do not tolerate retaliation against anyone who makes a report in good faith.

7. Legal compliance and reporting to authorities

We comply with applicable child safety laws and regulations. Where we confirm child sexual abuse material or child exploitation on our service, we report it to the relevant authorities. These include law enforcement in India, in line with the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012 and other applicable law, and the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) or another relevant regional authority.

8. If a child is in danger

If you believe a child is in immediate danger, or has been abused, exploited or trafficked, contact your local law enforcement first. You can also contact a child safety organisation:

  • India: Childline, phone 1098, and the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in.
  • Outside India: the NCMEC CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org, or your local child protection authority.
